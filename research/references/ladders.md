# Ladders: Worked Decomposition Patterns

An open catalogue of question shapes and the leaf sets they tend to earn.
Patterns guide; they never partition. A real question mixes shapes, and a
shape missing here is an invitation to decompose from first principles
under the deep-structure rule in `decompose`.

## Procedural fix

"MITM TLS fingerprint fix": what mechanism detects (the fingerprinting
method as a leaf), what the intercepting party controls, which mitigations
the mechanism's own documentation names, what each mitigation costs.
Constitutive sources exist (protocol specs, tool docs); close there.

## Canonical best practice

"C# ArrayPool<byte> buffer best practices": what the API contract
guarantees (BCL source: constitutive), what the owner recommends
(maintainer docs: attested), what practitioners believe (blogs: reported,
and the folk-belief leaf is Rival material when it contradicts the
contract). The misconception leaf is usually the most valuable one.

## Causal-historical

"Why did us-east-1 concentrate data centers": no peer-reviewed corpus;
leaves are dated primary records (announcements, filings, postmortems,
peering history). Test the obvious cause as a premise that evidence may
refute; the refutation (`Refuted` with its premise) often is the answer.

## Feasibility / speculative

"Can physical mirrors improve indoor 5G blind spots": decompose by the
physics that decides it (frequency band, wavelength versus reflector
size, penetration loss), plus a prior-art leaf (a serious version of the
idea usually exists under a different name; bridge vocabulary before
concluding novelty). Expect the answer to flip within scope: Boundary
section material.

## Topic survey

"ETH Zurich memory-based compute, non-von-Neumann": a topic, not a
question. First leaf: what the named group actually published (command
`/lit-review` for scholarly leaves). Remaining leaves: the governing
architectural principles the topic spans. This is the one shape where a
leaf legitimately delegates to a sibling skill wholesale.
