# Surface: marketing

Landing pages, portfolios, editorial, campaign pages, documentation homes.
User arrives with low commitment, leaves on first impression. Job:
comprehension in seconds, then one action.

## Hero and navigation

* **Hero fits first viewport.** Headline at most two lines on desktop,
  supporting sentence at most twenty words and four lines, primary action
  visible without scrolling. Value cannot be stated in twenty words: clarify
  copy.
* **At most four text elements in hero stack**: one optional small label,
  headline, supporting sentence, actions. Trust strips, taglines under
  buttons, pricing teasers, feature bullets, avatar rows move to sections
  below. Credibility logos sit under hero, never inside it.
* **Plan type scale and asset size together.** Long headline wraps to four
  lines at display scale: adjust size before shortening copy. Largest scale
  only for headlines of five words or fewer.
* **Cap hero top padding.** Keep content near top of first viewport; create
  breathing room with scale and asset size.
* **Navigation fits one line on desktop** at height not consuming meaningful
  share of viewport. Items do not fit: shorten labels, drop secondary items,
  or collapse.

## Sections

* **Each layout family appears once.** Assign every section distinct
  structural family: split, full-bleed, asymmetric grid, stacked editorial,
  horizontal scroll, quote, comparison. Page of eight sections uses at least
  four families; two sections sharing family read as one repeated section,
  flatten page.
* **Cap alternating splits at two consecutively.** Break third consecutive
  image-beside-text row with different family.
* **Grids hold exactly as many cells as there is content for.** Empty
  trailing cell: shape chosen before content counted; reshape grid.
* **Multi-cell grids need visual variation.** At least a third of cells
  carry real image, considered background, or pattern. Set of identical
  text-only cards: render as list.
* **Ration section labels.** Small uppercase label above heading once or
  twice per page for orientation, at most one per three sections, counting
  hero. Delete it when position on page already categorizes section.
* **Stack heading and explanation vertically.** Sentence under heading at
  readable measure, or give right column real content.
* **Lock theme.** One theme across page. At most one theme change, only as
  deliberate, strongly-transitioned composed device. Background tints within
  one theme family fine.

## Content density

* Per section: heading under about eight words, supporting paragraph under
  about twenty-five words, one asset or one action. More requires stated
  reason.
* No data dumps. Long specification table, thirty-row list, or full pricing
  matrix belongs behind disclosure, on own page, or reshaped into comparison
  of three to five highlights.
* Lists past about five items: different component: grouped columns, card
  grid, tabs, disclosure, or horizontally scrolling set.
* Quotes at most three lines; longer quote becomes case study. Attribution
  carries name and role, never bare first name.

## Copy audit before shipping

Re-read every visible string: headings, labels, buttons, body, captions,
alternative text, footer, error copy. Rewrite any string grammatically
broken, with unclear referent, depending on wordplay that does not survive
literal reading, or performing thoughtfulness. Plain, functional copy when
clever wording is slightly wrong. One register per page: technical
shorthand, editorial prose, or marketing punch.

## Imagery

Add small number of real images carrying real weight, even when minimalism
is goal; marketing surfaces are visual products.

Credibility logos: real marks in vector form, or, for invented brands,
simple generated monogram consistent with page style; row of styled text
names reads as placeholder. No category labels under logos: mark is
credibility.
